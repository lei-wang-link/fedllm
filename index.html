Project Introduction

Foundation models achieve strong performance across tasks, but domain adaptation is often limited by compute, data locality, and privacy constraints. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA reduce training cost by optimizing low-rank adapters, and federated learning enables privacy-preserving collaboration across distributed organizations. However, federated LoRA fine-tuning introduces distinct challenges in practice: biased server aggregation of factorized updates, inconsistent client initialization across rounds, and harmful cross-client interference under heterogeneous data.

This project consolidates our lab’s research agenda on federated fine-tuning with LoRA across three complementary directions: (1) improving global training stability via aggregation/initialization refinement (LoRA-FAIR), (2) handling heterogeneity via adaptive expert allocation and selection (FedLEASE), and (3) reducing cross-client interference via a personalized Rest-of-World LoRA mechanism with adaptive mixing (FedALT).

LoRA-FAIR: Federated LoRA Fine-Tuning with Aggregation and Initialization Refinement

ICCV 2025 · Jieming Bian*, Lei Wang*, Letian Zhang, Jie Xu

Paper Code arXiv

LoRA-FAIR targets two key issues in federated LoRA: server-side aggregation bias (naïve averaging of LoRA factors can deviate from the ideal global update) and client-side initialization lag (the need for consistent initialization across rounds). The method introduces a server correction term that improves aggregation efficiency and accuracy while maintaining LoRA’s computational and communication efficiency. Experiments on ViT and MLP-Mixer across large-scale datasets show consistent gains over strong baselines.

* indicates co-first authors.

Adaptive LoRA Experts Allocation and Selection for Federated Fine-Tuning (FedLEASE)

NeurIPS 2025 · Lei Wang*, Jieming Bian*, Letian Zhang, Jie Xu

Paper Code arXiv

A single LoRA module often struggles under heterogeneous client domains. FedLEASE addresses: (1) how many LoRA experts to use and how to allocate them across clients, and (2) how each client can selectively leverage the best experts. FedLEASE adaptively clusters clients based on representation similarity to allocate domain-specific experts, and introduces an adaptive top-M Mixture-of-Experts mechanism so each client can choose how many experts to use. Extensive experiments show strong improvements while keeping communication efficient.

* indicates co-first authors.

FedALT: Federated Fine-Tuning through Adaptive Local Training with Rest-of-World LoRA

AAAI 2026 · Jieming Bian, Lei Wang, Letian Zhang, Jie Xu

Paper Code arXiv

Federated LoRA fine-tuning can suffer from cross-client interference when aggregation mixes incompatible updates. FedALT departs from FedAvg-style initialization by letting each client continue training its own LoRA while incorporating shared knowledge through a separate Rest-of-World (RoW) LoRA component. An adaptive mixer learns input-specific weightings between the individual and RoW components, conceptually connected to Mixture-of-Experts. Experiments on NLP benchmarks show improved local adaptation and personalization without sacrificing efficiency.
